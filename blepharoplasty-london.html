Blepharoplasty in London with Dr Amynah Goawalla

Blepharoplasty removes or repositions excess eyelid skin and fat. As an oculoplastic surgeon, Dr Goawalla looks at the eyelids, the eye surface and your expectations together.

What the operation involves

For the upper eyelids, an incision is made along the lid crease and skin and fat are removed, so the scar sits in the natural fold. For the lower eyelids, the incision is just below the lashes or inside the lid, and fat is repositioned or removed, sometimes with a little skin.

Anaesthetic and time

Upper eyelid blepharoplasty can be done under local or general anaesthetic, depending on what is appropriate, safe and suits you. Lower eyelid blepharoplasty is usually done under general anaesthetic. The operation takes roughly 45 minutes to 2.5 hours. Most people go home the same day. Tell your surgeon about medicines that reduce blood clotting, such as aspirin, warfarin or apixaban.

Recovery

Most people take one to two weeks off work, depending on their job. You can drive once your eye pads are off and you are comfortable that your vision is not blurred. Bruising and redness can take several weeks to fade. Puffy eyelids and irritated or watery eyes for a few weeks are common.

Risks

Risks include over-correction, under-correction, asymmetry, unexpected scar formation and dry eye symptoms. Results cannot be guaranteed, and some people need a further operation.

Questions patients ask

Is blepharoplasty the same as ptosis surgery?

No. Blepharoplasty treats excess skin and fat. Ptosis surgery treats a drooping upper eyelid itself. Some people need one, some the other, and some both.

How long before I look normal?

Bruising on average takes one to two weeks to resolve. Scars can feel tight and look red for several weeks.
