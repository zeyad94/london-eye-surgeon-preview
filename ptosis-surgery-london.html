Eyelids

Heavy, or lifted?

Ptosis surgery in London with Dr Amynah Goawalla

Ptosis is drooping of the upper eyelid, in one or both eyes. A low lid can cut into the top of your vision as well as change how you look.

Call 07533 983835Email PA@londoneyesurgeon.ukAll clinics

What people notice

  • Difficulty keeping the eyelids open
  • An aching brow from constantly raising the lids
  • Eyestrain, and tiredness when reading
  • Concern about appearance

Why it happens in adults

  • Ageing or injury affecting the eyelid muscles or nerves
  • Long-term contact lens wear
  • Cataract surgery or other eye operations
  • A large cyst or swelling weighing the lid down
  • Rarely, muscle or nerve conditions

Assessment first

Because a droop can have different causes, the consultation looks at the lid, the muscles and nerves that lift it, and your vision before any surgery is discussed. A sudden droop needs urgent assessment: contact NHS 111.

Risks

Eyelid surgery can cause bruising and swelling, dry or irritated eyes, scarring, asymmetry and difficulty closing the eyes. Results cannot be guaranteed. Dr Goawalla explains the risks for your own procedure.

Questions patients ask

Can ptosis follow cataract surgery?

Yes. Cataract surgery and other eye operations are among the recognised causes of a drooping upper lid.

Is a sudden droop an emergency?

Yes. A sudden droop needs urgent assessment, or advice from NHS 111.
