Eyelids

Turned, or level?

Entropion and ectropion in London with Dr Amynah Goawalla

An eyelid that turns in rubs the lashes against the eye. One that turns out leaves the eye exposed. Both are usually linked to ageing, and both can be corrected.

Entropion: turning in

In entropion, the eyelashes rub against the eye and can introduce infection onto the surface of the eye or, worse, cause abrasive trauma to the cornea (the front window of the eye).

Ectropion: turning out

The lower eyelid droops or sags away from the eye. It can cause a sore, red eye, difficulty closing the eye, watering, a dry gritty feeling and more infections. It is usually due to the muscles around the eye weakening with age, and less commonly to long-term eye drops, facial nerve problems, a lump on the lid or scarring.

Treatment

Drops and ointment can protect the eye to some degree, but only for a while. To protect your eye and your vision, surgery treats the eyelid laxity, restores the normal position of the eyelid and re-establishes normal eyelid function, opening and closing. There is no need to wait until symptoms are severe: your eyelids protect your eyesight. Avoid rubbing the eyes, which can make ectropion worse.

Risks

Eyelid surgery can cause bruising and swelling, bleeding and infection. The problem can occasionally return. Dr Goawalla explains the risks for your own procedure.

Questions patients ask

When is it urgent?

Very red eyes, eye pain, changes in vision, light sensitivity, or a headache with nausea need urgent assessment. Come and see Dr Goawalla for prompt expert examination, investigation, diagnosis and management as appropriate.
