Treatments

Cataract and lens surgery, eyelid surgery, eye surface conditions, laser treatment and neuro-ophthalmology. Each page explains what is involved and the risks.

Cataract and lenses

  • Cataract surgeryA cataract is the eye’s natural lens turning cloudy. Dr Goawalla offers standard and complex cataract surgery, with the lens chosen around your eyes and the distances that matter to you.
  • Lens implantsChoosing a lens is a personal decision. Reading, screens, driving and how you feel about glasses all matter, alongside the health and measurements of your eyes.

Eyelids

  • BlepharoplastyBlepharoplasty removes or repositions excess eyelid skin and fat. As an oculoplastic surgeon, Dr Goawalla looks at the eyelids, the eye surface and your expectations together.
  • Ptosis surgeryPtosis is drooping of the upper eyelid, in one or both eyes. A low lid can cut into the top of your vision as well as change how you look.
  • Entropion and ectropionAn eyelid that turns in rubs the lashes against the eye. One that turns out leaves the eye exposed. Both are usually linked to ageing, and both can be corrected.
  • Eyelid lumps and cystsMost eyelid lumps are harmless. A few settle by themselves, some need removing, and all need checking properly.

Eye surface

  • Dry eyesDry eyes happen when you do not make enough tears, they dry up too quickly, or the surface of the eye is inflamed or exposed. When simple lubricating eye drops have not helped, a specialist looks into the cause.
  • Watery eyesWatering that keeps getting in the way of everyday life usually has a cause that can be found: a blocked tear duct, an eyelid out of position, or even dry eyes.

Laser

  • YAG laserMonths or years after cataract surgery, vision can mist over again. Sometimes it is the thin capsule behind the new lens clouding, and a quick laser treatment clears it.
  • SLT laser and MIGSDrops for eye pressure control, or controlled eye pressure off drops. SLT laser, or MIGS if cataract surgery is being undertaken with Dr Goawalla.

Laser and nerves

  • Neuro-ophthalmologySometimes the answer lies behind the eye: along the pathway of the optic nerves, through the eye socket or in the brain, or in the nerves that move the eyes, the eyelids or the pupils.
